AI-native operating model: organize the business around connected delivery.

AI-native means designing how information, work and responsibility move through the entire business. Acquired firms join a shared operating system: connected records, standardized workflows, centralized delivery support and consistent quality control. AI is part of that delivery method, with experienced professionals accountable for the work.

One infrastructure.

Connect client documents, bookkeeping records, tax work, invoices and payment status. Standardize data and permissions, with a common client portal and workflow queue. Preserve the context needed for a reliable handover.

Automation with professional review.

Use AI for document intake, draft categorization, reconciliation support and routine follow-up. Route exceptions and consequential decisions to qualified people; keep source records, review history and clear sign-off responsibilities.

Shared service and quality standards.

Centralize repeatable delivery support, onboarding and workflow management. Local leaders own client continuity; the platform defines standards, review procedures and escalation paths. Measure turnaround, rework and retention before expanding automation.

This is the proposed operating model. Productivity gains must be demonstrated in live delivery; the financial calculator retains its existing assumptions.

Growth strategy: acquire, integrate, improve and repeat.

  1. Acquire and preserve continuity. Select an initial firm, verify its records and service obligations, and agree a transition with the owner and delivery team.
  2. Integrate the operating foundation. Bring client records and workflows onto shared systems; establish access controls, service calendars and review responsibilities before automating delivery.
  3. Improve productivity and expand services. Deploy automation in repeatable workflows, monitor quality and capacity, and offer financial operations, invoicing and advisory where client needs and team capabilities support them.
  4. Scale a proven playbook. Use evidence from completed integrations to guide subsequent acquisitions. Expand central support and professional review capacity alongside the client base.

The phases describe implementation order and readiness gates, not a new acquisition timetable or funding projection.

Investment risks

Retention & integration

A client book can shrink after transition. Delayed margin improvements reduce cash available for debt service and new acquisitions.

Debt & liquidity

Financing may arrive later or cost more. Adverse assumptions can prevent milestone funding or cause a cash shortfall; the calculator identifies these constraints.

Exit & execution

Exit valuation and buyer demand are uncertain. The price and economics of each purchased business depend on its service mix, retained partner interest and transition terms.
