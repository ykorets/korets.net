01 / Investment thesis

Build a platform from recurring client relationships.

Acquire

Buy a client book.

Acquire small CPA practices with established revenue. Underwrite purchase price, retention, transition support and financing at the individual firm level.

Integrate

Improve the operating model.

Consolidate workflows, standardize delivery and introduce automation. The model assumes EBITDA margins rise gradually from 25% to 45% over 12 months.

Compound

Reinvest cash, then distribute.

Use operating cash flow to fund further purchases. From year five, distribute 30% of surplus cash; model an exit at year eight.

Cash reserves and acquisition capacity limit purchase pace. Financing terms must be negotiated; the base case assumes no seller equity issuance.

The first 24 months: a launch within the cash budget.

The current base model uses a $1.5M seed commitment in KPI tranches and produces three purchases in year one and one in year two. The five-purchase launch remains an ambition; the current budget funds four with the required cash reserve. From month 25, acquisition capacity is up to four firms per year, funded by operating cash generation and acquisition debt; no further equity round is assumed. Actual purchases remain limited by available cash.

Revenue and EBITDA in the tables are annualized rates at each year end, not the amounts earned during the whole year. Client equivalents use $5,000 annual fees, including $1,400 of assumed additional services. EBITDA is after central overhead and customer acquisition costs, before interest and taxes; it is not net profit.

A slower acquisition pace after the launch

Each comparison has the same first 24 months and $1.5M seed commitment. Only acquisition capacity from month 25 changes; slower capacity is a sensitivity, not a new agreed target. All retain 25% entry → 45% integrated practice EBITDA margins, 12% annual new revenue less 8% revenue churn, bank access from month 25 and an 8× EBITDA exit. Central overhead is $25K/month for the first 24 months ($600K). The base stays at $25K/month in year three and grows 10% annually from year four; the base does not add a separate per-firm overhead allowance. Staffing capacity and margins require operating evidence. Taxes, capital expenditure and working-capital timing are excluded.

06 / Deployment of the pre-seed

Capital goes into the first acquisitions and integration.

The model starts with $600K in cash against a $1.5M seed commitment. Further $450K tranches are drawn only after completed-month operating milestones are met. Purchases begin in month three, subject to a six-month platform overhead reserve. Total central overhead is $25K/month for the first 24 months; annual growth starts in month 37.

This is modeled first-year cash use, not an approved spending budget. Debt payments and operating cash generation are reflected in the closing cash balance.

08 / Investment risks

Retention & integration

A client book can shrink after transition. Delayed margin improvements reduce cash available for debt service and new acquisitions.

Debt & liquidity

Financing may arrive later or cost more. Adverse assumptions can prevent milestone funding or cause a cash shortfall; the calculator identifies these constraints.

Exit & execution

Exit valuation and buyer demand are uncertain. Actual CPA ownership rules, deal terms and tax structure need transaction-specific review.
