Investment overview

A $1M pre-seed investment for 50% ownership in a CPA acquisition platform. Understand the thesis, capital deployment and investor economics.

The starting deal$1,000,000 → 50%Pre-seed · $2M post-money · $1M pre-money
01 / Investment thesis

Build a platform from recurring client relationships.

Acquire

Buy a client book.

Acquire small CPA practices with established revenue. Underwrite purchase price, retention, transition support and financing at the individual firm level.

Integrate

Improve the operating model.

Consolidate workflows, standardize delivery and introduce automation. The model assumes operating margins rise gradually from 25% to 45% over 12 months.

Compound

Reinvest cash, then distribute.

Use operating cash flow to fund further purchases. From year five, distribute 30% of surplus cash; model an exit at year eight.

06 / Deployment of the pre-seed

Capital goes into the first acquisitions and integration.

The model starts with $1M in platform cash. Purchases begin in month three, subject to a six-month platform overhead reserve. Monthly platform overhead begins at $20K and grows by 10% per year.

This is modeled first-year cash use, not an approved spending budget. Debt payments and operating cash generation are reflected in the closing cash balance.

07 / Operating roadmap

Prove each stage before expanding.

Stage 1Source, diligence and close initial practices
Stage 2Validate retention and margin improvements
Stage 3Secure debt capacity and repeat integration
Stage 4Distribute surplus and assess exit options

Accounting is the modeled revenue base. Construction back-office services and insurance cross-selling remain potential extensions; their revenue is not separately included in the base case.

08 / Investment risks

Retention & integration

A client book can shrink after transition. Delayed margin improvements reduce cash available for debt service and new acquisitions.

Debt & liquidity

Financing may arrive later or cost more. The downside preset runs out of cash; the calculator stops and identifies the funding gap.

Exit & execution

Exit valuation and buyer demand are uncertain. Actual CPA ownership rules, deal terms and tax structure need transaction-specific review.
