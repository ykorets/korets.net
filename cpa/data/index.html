Revenue, profit and cash answer different questions.

Revenue is the amount earned from clients. Platform EBITDA deducts practice operating costs, central overhead and customer acquisition costs, before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Cash after debt also deducts acquisition-loan principal and interest.

Year-end revenue and EBITDA are annualized run rates: the final month multiplied by twelve. They are not the amounts earned during that year. The exports give both measures. Cash after debt is before taxes, capital expenditure, working-capital changes, new purchases and distributions; it is not net profit or fully distributable cash.

Estimated client equivalents divide annualized revenue by $5,000 per year, including assumed additional services. They are not verified individual client counts. A ten-year horizon is a planning assumption, not a fund term or promised exit.

Keep sourced facts separate from planning assumptions.

Market observations retain their own years and units. A Census office is not necessarily a separate company. A publicly announced acquisition budget is not a completed transaction price. Undisclosed purchase prices remain unknown.

The Base assumes a $1.5M seed commitment, a later $2.5M equity round, bank access from month 25 and larger acquisitions from year three. Later targets use a $2M count-weighted average revenue, a 1.25× purchase multiple and a 40% integrated practice margin. This is an acquisition hypothesis; actual large-deal cash timing will vary.

The preferred strategy allows sellers to retain negotiated local equity. The current financial model assumes full ownership of the purchased businesses: minority distributions and future partner buyouts are not yet included. Seed-investor returns are left unavailable when later funding terms are unspecified.
