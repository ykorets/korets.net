Proposed acquisition profile. Start with owner-led practices around $300K–$1M annual fees, centered on the model’s $550K. Favor recurring bookkeeping and business tax work, a transferable team and client relationships, clean records, and a seller willing to support transition. This range is our screening assumption.

At $550K revenue, the model implies approximately 110 client equivalents at $5,000/year, or 153 at $3,600. Actual practices mix individual returns, business clients and advisory projects; count unique paying relationships and recurring fees during diligence. A sole owner doing most of the work can leave a replacement-cost problem even when reported owner earnings look high.

How many owners will sell?

There is evidence of succession pressure, but no verified count of upcoming sales. AICPA’s 2020 survey found 44% of responding sole/solo practitioners planned to retire within five years, and another 31% within six to ten years. Those windows started in 2020; they must not be restarted in 2026.

AICPA: succession survey context and dates. Retirement intentions among respondents are not completed sales, all-firm population shares or a California forecast.

Some practices transfer internally, merge, wind down or sell only a client book. We therefore do not multiply that survey percentage by all US firms and call the result “available targets.” A reliable 2026–2030 US or California ownership-transfer count remains unverified.

Planning sensitivity, not a market forecast: if 2–5% of the 2022 employer CPA-firm base came to market annually, that would mean roughly 1,000–2,490 US firms offered for sale, or 137–343 in California. This rate is hypothetical, excludes nonemployers and is before size, geography, quality, competition and financing filters. Four annual purchases in the Base case would require about 0.16–0.40% of that hypothetical US flow, or 1.17–2.92% of the California flow. The Unicorn case alone assumes capacity for 30 purchases per year from year 3: about 1.2–3.0% of the hypothetical US flow, or 8.7–21.9% of the California flow.

Acquisition targets and completed purchases. An acquisition target is a firm we may buy. In this model, a qualified acquisition target has passed initial checks for size, geography, service mix and financial fit, and its owner is open to discussing a sale. A completed purchase is a closed transaction.

Annual capacity is a model limit, not a promised number of purchases. The Base case completes 3 purchases in year 1 and 1 in year 2 with the $1.5M staged commitment; its capacity remains 4 per year. The conversion assumption is 10–20% from qualified acquisition target to completed purchase, so required qualified targets = purchases ÷ conversion. The initial sourcing pool must be larger. Both conversion and qualification criteria need validation against our own pipeline.

Normalize earnings before comparing. SDE adds back an owner’s compensation and selected discretionary expenses. A platform must pay someone to replace that owner. A 50% owner-earnings margin is not evidence of 50% EBITDA after market salaries, software, insurance and management.

Large-platform transactions are a different market. CBIZ’s completed Marcum transaction was approximately $2.3B in cash and stock; the non-attest business and the independent CPA business were acquired by separate entities. Dividing that headline by Marcum’s total disclosed revenue does not establish a clean small-practice or EBITDA multiple. Closing disclosure.

Financing is the largest mismatch in the first 24 months.

Accounting Practice Sales describes current transactions as primarily bank-funded, with seller financing, when present, often 10–20% of price over 3–5 years. This is a broker’s market observation, not a statistical guarantee. The owner sets 50% cash at closing and a 50% seller note as the best achievable planning terms. Our no-bank launch therefore needs 50% seller financing: a negotiated exception to validate, not a market norm. APS financing guide.

What is working in accounting platforms?

Observed operating patterns: specialized client niches, recurring fixed fees, dedicated delivery teams, common systems and higher-value advisory services. In the 2024 AICPA/CPA.com CAS survey, 206 self-selected practices reported 2023 results: median growth of 17%, median net client fees of $156,250 per professional, and only 10% using hourly billing as their primary method. This is a CAS sample, not the entire CPA market. Survey release and methodology.

Current reported a tax-preparation AI pilot covering 7,000 returns with average preparation-time savings of 31%. That is a company-reported task-level result, not a 31% reduction in all costs or a proven EBITDA uplift. Human review, client communication and professional responsibility remain part of delivery. Current, June 2026.

Proposed offer: $300/month for a tightly defined recurring service = $3,600/year. Reaching $5,000 requires an additional $1,400/year on average, or about 39% above the base subscription. Tax work and advisory can contribute, but the mix, attach rate and workload need testing. Do not promise unlimited complex tax or CFO services inside a $300 monthly package.

Margin test. A 45% practice EBITDA margin leaves $2,750 per $5,000 client for all practice operating costs. A $3,600 client leaves $1,980. Staff, replacement-owner compensation, software, review, insurance and local management must fit that budget before central overhead and acquisition marketing. Automation alone does not establish that this is achievable.

Why the Unicorn result is still a stretch case

The preset assumes a $1.5M seed commitment, an additional $5M equity round in month 25, 20% own cash + 30% bank + 50% seller note for purchases from month 25, up to 30 purchases annually thereafter, 45% integrated practice margins, 18% annual gross new revenue versus 6% revenue churn, and a 12× EBITDA exit. Funding and terms are uncommitted. The $5M growth round is included in business cash; its terms and the seed investor’s eventual return are outside this model.

Taxes, capital expenditure, working-capital timing, acquisition-price inflation and client-level delivery costs are not modeled explicitly. The model scales central overhead, but does not prove the staffing, senior-review or integration capacity. Acquisition closing costs reduce cash separately from its EBITDA measure. The accumulated cash and terminal valuation therefore need a more detailed operating and tax budget before investment use.

Assessment: a substantial accounting platform is plausible; this particular billion-dollar outcome is not yet validated. Treat the base case as a planning hypothesis and the Unicorn case as a statement of what must become true. A small share of a large market does not establish a credible route to that share.

The platform and the licensed CPA firm are different ownership questions.

California Business and Professions Code §5079 requires licensees to hold more than half of a licensed public-accountancy firm’s equity and majority voting rights. It also imposes owner-count and material-participation conditions. Investor ownership of a licensed firm must meet these requirements. California §5079.

An alternative practice structure separates an investor-owned services business from an appropriately licensed, independent CPA firm. CBIZ’s Marcum closing illustrates that separation; it does not make every proposed structure compliant. Define which entity provides each service, holds client contracts and employs staff, and how independence and fees work before the first transaction. The financial illustration does not establish a licensed-firm ownership structure.

Prove three acquisitions before underwriting hundreds.

Recommended investment sequence — proposed, not agreed terms. Keep the $1.5M milestone commitment and no-bank-first-24-month constraint. Use the first firms to validate actual economics, then negotiate the next capital and integration capacity from evidence.

  1. Before purchase one: confirm a compliant entity structure, transferable client contracts, a replacement-owner salary budget, and a seller who accepts the proposed financing. Rebuild the 24-month cash plan from those terms.
  2. Across the first three firms: measure cohort revenue retention, client and staff retention, collection timing, hours per client, realized fees, replacement costs, quality and normalized EBITDA. Separate price increases from real client growth.
  3. Before accelerating: demonstrate repeatable integration with named accountable managers, a qualified sourcing pipeline, and financing that is actually available. The model’s DSCR gates use practice EBITDA and exclude central overhead; also assess coverage after all platform costs.
  4. Before a Unicorn claim: replace blanket margin and productivity assumptions with a staffed operating plan, taxes, working capital, pricing evidence and financing commitments. Stress a lower exit multiple and delayed purchases.

Retain the existing funding gates as proposals until agreed. Consider requiring several completed months of evidence rather than one favorable month; this page does not silently change the calculator’s funding rules.

Source notes & definitions

Primary government data support market counts and revenue. Surveys describe their respondents. Company releases describe their own scale and technology results. Broker and marketplace data are transaction evidence with selection bias. All recommendations, acquisition filters, transfer-rate sensitivities and productivity improvements on this page are explicitly our assumptions.

Download the extracted public data and filters. Economic Census table EC2254BASIC: total tax status, US and California, NAICS 5412 and 541211. Revenue is published in thousands of dollars and converted to dollars. CBP uses all legal forms; employment bands count offices. Nonemployer totals use all legal forms and all receipt sizes. Different years and statistical units are not summed.

2022 employer revenue is a verified benchmark, not a claim about 2026 revenue. The Census API required a key during this research; the same agency’s open bulk files were used and checked directly. This page does not claim that these vintages are the latest available for every series.

CPA
Certified Public Accountant; a licensed professional. “CPA firm” here refers to the relevant accounting business, not the number of license holders.
CAS
Client Advisory Services: recurring outsourced accounting, controller and advisory work. Scope varies by practice.
SDE
Seller’s Discretionary Earnings: owner-oriented earnings with specified add-backs. Not interchangeable with EBITDA after replacement compensation.
NAICS
North American Industry Classification System. 5412 is the broad accounting-services group; 541211 is offices of certified public accountants.
Firm / establishment / nonemployer
A firm is a business organization; an establishment is a business location. A nonemployer has no paid employees and may be an owner working alone.
FTE
Full-time equivalent: a unit of staffing capacity. The sizing calculation above uses professional capacity, not total employees including administration.
APS / attest
APS can mean alternative practice structure; the broker Accounting Practice Sales is identified by its full name where relevant. Attest work includes professional assurance on financial information and carries licensing and independence requirements.
