Current: buy control and keep local partners invested.

Disclosed model. Current, formerly Crete Professionals Alliance, acquires majority stakes in accounting businesses. Its earlier investment announcement specifies investments in the non-attest businesses, using alternative practice structures. That establishes the broad acquisition perimeter, not the exact assets or percentage purchased in every transaction. Current’s acquisition description · May 2024 capital announcement.

Partner participation. Continuing partners retain equity in their local businesses. Current describes local-level rollover equity: sellers keep part of their economic exposure invested after a transaction. Its public description does not establish that every seller instead receives shares in the parent holding company. Exact percentages, voting protections and repurchase rights require deal documents. Current on local-level rollover equity.

Local operations. The firm keeps its local brand and leadership while the platform supplies technology and shared capabilities. In a 2024 interview, Josh Funk described centralized recruiting with the local partner choosing the hire, and central finance support replacing routine administrative work. Retained operational authority does not mean there is no controlling shareholder. Crete leadership interview, December 2024.

Scale and technology evidence. On June 2, 2026, Current reported almost 30 firms, more than 2,000 employees and over $500M annual revenue. Its tax AI pilot covered 7,000 returns and reported average preparation-time savings of 31%. These are company disclosures. A task-level time saving is not a company-wide cost reduction or an audited improvement in EBITDA. Current also described a long-term holding approach; that is not a contractual promise to any individual seller. June 2026 rebrand and operating disclosure.

The live homepage checked October 12, 2026 lists 54 firms, 2,500+ employees, 39 states and four countries. Its firm-count perimeter is not reconciled with the June release, so we do not derive an acquisition pace or average purchase price from the difference. Current homepage.

What did Current pay?

The reviewed public materials do not establish a standard purchase price, EBITDA multiple, purchased percentage, cash/seller-note split, platform fee or guaranteed dividend. Reuters reported a plan in June 2025 to invest over $500M in acquisitions over the following two years, financed from cash generation and external capital. That was a program budget, not a price for one firm or Current’s valuation. Reuters report, syndicated full text.

Our interpretation. The attractive seller proposition is partial liquidity today, a continuing leadership role and upside from a stronger local business. It can appeal to owners who want to grow, not only those retiring. Whether this improves our closing rate or returns remains to be demonstrated in our own negotiations.

Two Current firms: one brand, separate businesses.

Larson Gross

Larson Gross Assurance, PLLC is the independent licensed CPA firm providing attest services. Larson Gross Advisors, LLC and its subsidiaries provide tax and business consulting and are not licensed CPA firms. Their published disclosure describes separate ownership and liability under a shared brand. It does not disclose the acquisition price, Current’s exact interest or local partners’ cash receipts. Legal-entity disclosure.

Levy, Erlanger & Company

Levy, Erlanger & Company, LLP provides attest services as an independent licensed CPA firm. Levy Erlanger Advisors, LLC provides tax and business consulting and is not a licensed CPA firm. The page confirms the alternative practice structure; it does not publish price, partner percentages or service-fee terms. Legal-entity disclosure.

Application to us. A familiar firm name can cover two entities with different owners and responsibilities. Acquisition diligence must identify which entity owns each client contract, employs staff, earns the revenue and bears the liability. We should not price the entire branded practice as if every activity were transferred to our holding company.

Ascend: local leadership with a defined acquisition perimeter.

LevitZacks · San Diego, California

Transaction effective March 1, 2024. Ascend, backed by Alpine Investors, added LevitZacks’ advisory practice. LevitZacks Corporation, affiliated with Ascend, provides tax, advisory and other non-attest services; a newly established, independently owned LevitZacks CPAs, LLP provides attest services. The announcement identified nine partners and 51 staff joining the platform, and local leaders Victor Ramsauer and Sue Watson. Deal terms were not disclosed. Ascend announcement, March 15, 2024.

Application to us. This is a documented California structure worth examining for a mixed-service target. It does not establish that our proposed documents comply or that this much larger practice has $550K-firm economics. Confirm the non-attest revenue purchased, the licensed professional successor and the services agreement before agreeing a price.

Goering & Granatino / GG Advisors · Kansas

Transaction effective October 1, 2023. Goering & Granatino, P.A. remains the independent licensed attest firm; GG Advisors LLC provides tax, business advisory and other non-attest services. The business added 40 professionals to Ascend. The announcement identifies Amber Goering as GG Advisors’ CEO and describes centralized investment in leadership, recruiting, technology and delivery capacity. It does not state an acquisition price, purchased percentage or local equity split. Ascend announcement, October 3, 2023.

Application to us. Existing operators can keep leading their business while gaining central support. That fits our proposed seller profile. Retained leadership and retained equity are separate terms: this announcement is not evidence that every Ascend seller keeps the same local minority stake as Current describes.

CBIZ / Marcum: acquisition value and service-contract revenue.

Closed November 1, 2024. CBIZ acquired Marcum’s non-attest business. Separately, the independent firm CBIZ CPAs acquired Marcum’s attest business. The closing announcement described a cash-and-stock transaction valued at approximately $2.3B and expected combined annualized CBIZ revenue of about $2.8B. Those are different measures. The headline is not a small-practice price multiple or the cost of buying an audit license. Closing announcement.

Scale reference. At the July 2024 announcement, CBIZ described more than 135,000 combined clients; Marcum alone had over 35,000 clients and approximately $1.2B annual revenue. These businesses span much richer service mixes than our proposed $300/month core offer. The counts show that large client platforms exist, not that their client economics transfer to us. Announcement-date client and revenue figures.

How the services company earns revenue

The 2025 Form 10-K/A describes administrative service agreements (ASAs) with four independent CPA firms and $651.2M of related fees in 2025. Services include office space, systems and personnel. The CPA firms retain professional control of attest work. These fees are service revenue, not pure profit, and not a disclosed universal royalty percentage.

The filing describes variable-interest-entity accounting and CBIZ as primary beneficiary, but excludes the separate CPA-firm operations and balances as immaterial while recognizing its service fees. Legal ownership, accounting consolidation and the cash available to investors therefore need separate analysis. CBIZ 2025 Form 10-K/A, regulation and Note 1.

Application to us. Real services can support substantial platform revenue. Our fee must cover actual work and leave the local business viable. There is no basis here for assuming a 10% management fee or taking all of an independent CPA firm’s residual profit. Internal fees within our consolidated subsidiaries must be eliminated when calculating group revenue and EBITDA.

Baker Tilly / Moss Adams: principal equity alongside institutional capital.

Structure announced April 21, 2025. The planned combination placed audit operations in Baker Tilly US, LLP and tax, advisory and other services in Baker Tilly Advisory Group, LP. Hellman & Friedman and Valeas were increasing their advisory-business investments, with principals holding equity alongside them. The announcement did not state a purchase price or each principal’s ownership allocation. The source’s current disclosure also distinguishes the licensed CPA firm from the nonlicensed advisory entities. Baker Tilly announcement and entity disclosure.

Application to us. Professional leaders and outside investors can participate in the advisory business while the regulated audit firm follows its own ownership requirements. This is a later-stage architecture reference. It is not evidence that we can purchase 100% of a licensed California CPA firm merely by employing a CPA, or that our local sellers should all receive holding-company shares.

The structure we would adapt.

  1. Investors and founder own the holding company. It acquires control of eligible bookkeeping, tax and advisory businesses. A continuing partner may retain a negotiated local stake.
  2. Local firms keep accountable operating leaders. They use shared technology, recruiting, administration and quality processes. Commercial decision rights, budgets and service charges are documented.
  3. Regulated engagements follow a separate professional route. Where needed, an appropriately owned and licensed CPA firm contracts for the work and retains professional control. An external provider may be simpler when this work is small.
  4. Measure economics at each level. Local profit after platform charges; platform profit after service-delivery costs; group profit after eliminating internal charges; then the cash and value attributable to the shares we actually own.

This is our proposed architecture, not an approved structure for a particular candidate. California ownership rules: §5079. See the service and regulated-engagement definitions.

A continuing partner’s stake: upside without a fixed template.

Our preference: leave a meaningful stake with an operator who stays and grows the firm. The retained percentage, salary, authority, distribution policy and future buyout are individual deal terms. A full acquisition with a credible management plan remains possible. This is not a change to the seed investor’s illustrative participation.

Closing costs, working capital, tax and integration costs are additional. The seller note is debt, not retained ownership. The buyer’s note must be serviced from cash legally available to the borrower; a local minority owner does not automatically fund it.

Can a seller keep the same income with 20% ownership?

Only if the relevant distributable profit grows enough and the distribution policy supports it. Keeping 20% requires five times the former total profit pool to preserve the former 100% share. For example, $100K × 100% before the sale equals $500K × 20% afterward. This assumes comparable profit definitions, full distribution and unchanged salary treatment. It is arithmetic, not a forecast.

A fivefold increase in EBITDA does not necessarily create fivefold partner distributions: interest, tax, reinvestment, cash reserves, platform charges and where the acquisition debt sits all matter. Salary for continuing work must be separated from dividends. If growth requires new equity, percentage ownership may also change.

The current margin assumption alone — 25% improving to 45% on unchanged revenue — produces 1.8× EBITDA, not 5×. Reaching 5× through those margins would also require about 2.78× revenue, before changes in other costs. Technology must create measurable capacity, growth or savings; it is not a promised fivefold return.

What the calculator would need next. Add acquired ownership and retained partner equity per deal; separate operating compensation from distributions; locate the seller note at its borrower; deduct minority claims from investor-attributable cash and exit value. Buying less equity reduces the initial purchase outlay but also reduces our economic participation. We should compare that trade-off before replacing the current full-ownership illustration.
