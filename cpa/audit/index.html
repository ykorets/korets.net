Transparent assumptions. Reproducible calculations.

The earlier model’s audit statements and test counts do not certify this rebuilt model. This page reports the checks performed for this version. It is not an independent financial audit or a market valuation opinion.

02 / Cash-flow engine

From acquired practices to investor receipts.

1. Fund and acquire

Start with $600K of a $1.5M commitment. Draw two further $450K tranches only after the modeled operating milestones are met. Add an optional funding round at its selected month. Buy no more than the acquisition capacity, and only when cash covers the down payment, closing costs and reserve. From month 25, a separate acquisition capacity can apply. Above 12 acquisitions per year, multiple purchases can close in a month; scheduled slots are spread across each year. Additional per-firm platform overhead is applied from acquisition.

2. Operate and service debt

Compound revenue monthly using new-client growth minus churn. Ramp EBITDA margins over the integration period. Deduct debt payments, customer acquisition costs and platform overhead.

3. Distribute and exit

At eligible year ends, pay a percentage of cash above the reserve. Calculate net equity at exit, allocate it to the investor and solve IRR from the dated cash flows.

03 / Firm economics

Revenue and operating margin

Monthly revenue growth = (new-client growth − churn) / 12 Margin at age t = entry + (target − entry) × min(t / ramp, 1) Annualized platform EBITDA = Σ(firm revenue × margin) − annualized overhead − annualized customer acquisition costs

New-client growth and churn are annual rates applied monthly; compounding means realized annual growth differs from the simple net rate. New acquisitions begin at the entry margin in their first month.

04 / Acquisition financing

Monthly amortization

Monthly rate r = annual interest / 12 Number of payments n = loan term × 12 Payment = principal × r / [1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] If r = 0: payment = principal / n New balance = max(0, prior balance + interest − payment)

Bank and seller notes amortize separately. A final payment is capped at the outstanding balance plus interest; debt service stops after repayment. No bank borrowing is permitted in months 1–24; seller financing is permitted. Bank availability from month 25 is an assumption. Base purchases have no seller equity or payment standby.

05 / Ownership and exit

Seed ownership assumes full funding.

Illustrative investor ownership after full seed funding = 50% Founder allocation = 30% Future capital reserve = 100% − founder allocation − seed allocation New allocation = new capital / valuation including full reserve Reserve remaining = prior reserve − new allocation Seed participation remains unchanged; reject rounds exceeding reserve Enterprise value = max(0, platform EBITDA) × exit multiple Exit equity = max(0, enterprise value − debt + cash balance) Investor exit = ownership at exit × equity

Each dividend uses ownership at the month of payment. The remaining initial ownership is not treated as another investment round. A later round is optional and must fit the reserve. Percentages use a fixed planning basis including the reserve, not currently issued shares. The illustrative seed allocation is not a target or agreed deal terms.

06 / Investor returns

MOIC and IRR answer different questions.

Total receipts = investor distributions + investor exit Investment gain (pre-tax) = total receipts − seed capital actually funded MOIC = total receipts / seed capital actually funded IRR solves: Σ cash flowₘ / (1 + IRR)^(m/12) = 0

The initial $600K is a negative cash flow at month zero; each later $450K seed draw is a negative investor cash flow in its actual funding month. MOIC and investment gain use capital actually paid in. If the seed commitment is not fully drawn, distributions and exit-return metrics are withheld because partial-funding ownership is not specified. Earlier distributions increase IRR relative to receiving the same total amount entirely at exit. No additional investor contributions are included when an optional third-party round is raised.

24-month launch funding

The launch budget runs 24 monthly periods using the current acquisition and operating assumptions, with no distributions, sale proceeds or later equity raise. Practice EBITDA is summed across the months; the main investor table uses month-end annualized revenue and EBITDA instead.

Proposed seed drawdowns are 40% at launch, 30% after one acquired firm and practice DSCR ≥ 1.25×, and 30% after two firms with revenue-weighted practice EBITDA margin ≥ 35% and DSCR ≥ 1.25×. A completed month earns each KPI; funding arrives at the start of the next month. A stress case adds a three-month payment delay. Unmet gates remain undrawn.

The full-pace capital requirement removes only the cash constraint to generate the capacity schedule, then finds the upfront amount that preserves the overhead reserve after every acquisition and at each month end. The funded result is run again through the normal acquisition rules. Launch checks are available as launch-checks.cjs.

Scenario scale and Unicorn calculations

Four presets share the monthly engine. Unicorn uses 10 years, up to 30 acquisitions per year from month 25, 18% gross revenue growth less 6% revenue churn, 45% integrated practice margin, 12× exit multiple,$1,000 extra monthly central overhead per firm and no distributions. An assumed $5M round at a $25M valuation including the reserve consumes 20% reserve. These are editable hypotheses, not market benchmarks.

The Unicorn threshold is modeled equity value of $1B, after debt and cash, not enterprise value alone. Clients are annualized revenue divided by a $5K annual fee assumption ($300/month ×12 = $3,600 base fees plus $1,400 of assumed annual additional services), not unique-client census data. Static target sizing holds cash/debt, net platform margin and average revenue per firm fixed. It does not create a funded plan. Tests: scenario-checks.cjs.

09 / Practical limits

Not modeled

Taxes, exit fees, transaction-specific working capital, preferred returns, liquidation preferences and seller equity dilution.

Assumed

Debt availability, steady client growth/churn, integration capacity, EBITDA-margin improvements and an executable exit multiple.

Cash failure

A negative cash balance stops the simulation. The funding gap is shown, while exit proceeds, MOIC and IRR are withheld.
